Methodological literature

Peer-reviewed references for SPiCT, JABBA and related methods

SPiCT and JABBA are widely documented state-space surplus production model frameworks in fisheries assessment. SPiCT is based on a continuous-time stochastic production model formulation. JABBA is a Bayesian state-space biomass-dynamics framework implemented in JAGS. The peer-reviewed literature includes core model descriptions, practical guidance, time-varying productivity developments, selectivity extensions and risk-equivalence frameworks.

Core references

Pedersen, M.W. and Berg, C.W. 2017. A stochastic surplus production model in continuous time. Fish and Fisheries, 18(2): 226–243. https://doi.org/10.1111/faf.12174

  • Core SPiCT reference paper.
  • Introduces the continuous-time stochastic surplus production model.
  • Separates process variation in stock dynamics from observation error in biomass indices.
  • Includes fisheries dynamics so that uncertainty in catches can be propagated into estimated parameters and management quantities.

Winker, H., Carvalho, F. and Kapur, M. 2018. JABBA: Just Another Bayesian Biomass Assessment. Fisheries Research, 204: 275–288. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fishres.2018.03.010

  • Core JABBA reference paper.
  • Presents an open-source Bayesian state-space biomass-dynamics assessment framework.
  • Includes alternative production functions, multiple CPUE series, additional observation variance, retrospective diagnostics and projections.
  • Case study: South Atlantic swordfish.

JABBA-Select extension

Winker, H., Carvalho, F., Thorson, J.T., Kell, L.T., Parker, D., Kapur, M., Sharma, R., Booth, A.J. and Kerwath, S.E. 2020. JABBA-Select: Incorporating life history and fisheries’ selectivity into surplus production models. Fisheries Research, 222: 105355. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fishres.2019.105355

  • Extends JABBA to account for fisheries selectivity and life-history information.
  • Distinguishes exploitable biomass from spawning biomass.
  • Provides a bridge between conventional surplus production models and age-structured production models.
  • Case study: South African silver kob.

Hierarchical assessment frameworks and risk equivalence

Kell, L.T., Cardinale, M., Mosqueira, I., Winker, H. and Sharma, R. 2025. Transforming Data and Capacity-Limited Stock Assessment: Achieving Risk Equivalence with Hierarchical Assessment Frameworks and Auxiliary Data. Sustainability, 17(21): 9383. https://doi.org/10.3390/su17219383

  • Methodological synthesis and extension.
  • Proposes a hierarchical assessment framework (HAF) built around Bayesian state-space biomass dynamic models.
  • Uses JABBA with auxiliary data to demonstrate the framework.
  • Evaluates risk equivalence across data-rich and data-limited assessment tiers.
  • Integrates auxiliary information including fishing mortality indicators, depletion information and length-based metrics into JABBA-type biomass dynamic assessments.
  • Uses simulation testing conditioned on 82 assessed Northeast Atlantic stocks.

Comparative evaluation and uncertainty literature

Kell, L.T., Sharma, R. and Winker, H. Artefact and Artifice: evaluation of the skill of catch-only methods for classifying stock status.

  • Comparative paper on assessment skill and classification performance.
  • Explores JABBA in a catch-only configuration alongside other catch-only assessment methods.
  • Relevant to model plausibility, structural uncertainty, stock-status misclassification risk and comparative assessment frameworks.

Good-practice reference for SPiCT and JABBA

Kokkalis, A., Berg, C.W., Kapur, M.S., Winker, H., Jacobsen, N.S., Taylor, M.H., Ichinokawa, M., Miyagawa, M., Medeiros-Leal, W., Nielsen, J.R. and Mildenberger, T.K. 2024. Good practices for surplus production models. Fisheries Research, 275: 107010. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fishres.2024.107010

  • Provides good-practice recommendations for applying surplus production models.
  • Discusses SPiCT and JABBA.
  • Covers input data, priors, diagnostics, simulation testing and use of SPMs for advice.

Management strategy evaluation and SPiCT performance testing

Mildenberger, T.K., Berg, C.W., Kokkalis, A., Nielsen, J.R., Punt, A.E. and co-authors. Full citation to be completed once bibliographic details are verified.

  • Evaluates SPiCT within a management strategy evaluation framework.
  • Tests harvest control rules and robustness of SPiCT-based management advice.
  • Links SPiCT to operational management procedures and risk-performance evaluation.

Time-varying productivity extensions

Chang, Y.-J., Winker, H., Sculley, M. and Hsu, J. 2020. Evaluation of the status and risk of overexploitation of the Pacific billfish stocks considering non-stationary population processes. Deep Sea Research Part II: Topical Studies in Oceanography, 175: 104707. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dsr2.2019.104707

  • Uses the JABBA framework with non-stationary population processes.
  • Explores implications for biomass trajectories and management reference points.
  • Illustrates extension of Bayesian surplus production models beyond stationary production assumptions.

Mildenberger, T.K. et al. 2025. Estimating time-varying productivity and reference points: A case of North Sea demersal fish stocks. Fish and Fisheries. https://doi.org/10.1111/faf.12910

  • Applies a modified SPiCT-type framework with time-varying productivity.
  • Relevant for cases where productivity and reference points are not stationary.

Classical surplus production models

  • Schaefer, M.B. (1954). Some aspects of the dynamics of populations important to the management of commercial marine fisheries. Inter-American Tropical Tuna Commission Bulletin, 1: 27–56.

  • Schaefer, M.B. (1957). A study of the dynamics of the fishery for yellowfin tuna in the eastern tropical Pacific Ocean. Inter-American Tropical Tuna Commission Bulletin, 2: 247–285.

  • Fox, W.W. (1970). An exponential surplus-yield model for optimizing exploited fish populations. Transactions of the American Fisheries Society, 99: 80–88.

  • Pella, J.J. and Tomlinson, P.K. (1969). A generalized stock production model. Inter-American Tropical Tuna Commission Bulletin, 13: 421–496.

State-space and Bayesian biomass dynamic models

  • Meyer, R. and Millar, R.B. (1999). BUGS in Bayesian stock assessments. Canadian Journal of Fisheries and Aquatic Sciences, 56: 1078–1087.

Fishery performance and participatory MSE (FPAT)

De Alessi, M., Anderson, C., Carruthers, T., Hordyk, A., Sock, F. and Gutierrez, N.L. 2026. The Fisheries Performance Assessment Toolkit (FPAT): a practical manual. FAO Fisheries and Aquaculture Technical Paper 737. Rome, FAO (in press).

  • Manual for the FPAT cycle: benchmarking with Fishery Performance Indicators and a governance module, then MSE of management options in the FPAT App (openMSE).
  • Data guidance, scoresheets, interview guide and workshop formats; lessons from the Coastal Fisheries Initiative.

Anderson, J.L., Anderson, C.M., Chu, J., Meredith, J., Asche, F., Sylvia, G., Smith, M.D. et al. 2015. The fishery performance indicators: a management tool for triple bottom line outcomes. PLoS ONE, 10(5): e0122809. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0122809
