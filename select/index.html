What the method selector does, and why it matters

The selector helps users decide which assessment methods suit a stock before investing time in any one of them. It lists methods whose data requirements are met, flags assumptions their situation may violate, and names methods needing data they do not yet have. It is a screening aid in the spirit of FishPath: it narrows options and flags risks, and does not replace diagnostics or working-group review.

Matching method to data

A method used on data it was not designed for gives confident-looking results that can mislead management: catch-only methods fail on under-reported catch, single-fleet length methods on pooled gears, and production models on hyperstable CPUE.

Warnings, not just a yes or no

Each result carries a status. Do not use means a core assumption is violated by the data described, whereas caution means a method can be used with the checks and sensitivity runs listed on its card.

A route through the whole toolbox

Results link to each method's landing page, suggest data-preparation tools needed beforehand, and point to advice steps such as FLRef and FPAT, so users see what to do next rather than only which model to try.

Assessment methods

Single-species assessment tools fall into four groups, from empirical indicators needing little data to age-structured models needing the most. Tools can serve several purposes and each sits under its main use. Simulation, single-species MSE and multispecies tools sit alongside, and arrows show common next steps or complements rather than a required order.

Use the method selector

Answers are pre-set to sensible defaults, and users change any of them to describe their own stock. Questions about the index switch off when users select “None” for abundance indices.
