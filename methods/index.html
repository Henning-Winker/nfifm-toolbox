Assessment methods

Assessment methodsDraft

Numerous stock assessment methods are available, and they differ in quality, in the data they require, and in how widely they are applied. The toolbox maps this landscape and provides a small set of well-documented FAO tools, supported with training, applications and manuals.

Current tools and FAO tools

Methods already in use have real strengths, and FAO tools add documentation and support. The comparison below sets out the trade-offs of each.

Current practiceEstablished and regional tools

Methods already in use in countries and regions, many developed locally or over several decades.

Strengths
  • Familiar to analysts and institutions, so teams can start without retraining
  • Long records of results support continuity of time series and comparison with past assessments
  • Already embedded in national and regional reporting routines
  • Suited to local fisheries and data where they were developed
Limitations
  • Documentation, assumptions and outputs vary, so results are hard to compare between countries
  • Some tools are older software with limited maintenance and limited treatment of uncertainty
  • Limited guidance on which data conditions suit each method
  • Training and support depend on individual institutes and a few experts
Toolbox approachFAO tools

A small set of tools the FAO toolbox recommends and supports with resources.

Strengths
  • Documented, maintained and tested, with training materials, Shiny apps and manuals
  • Outputs are consistent and align with FAO stock status reporting
  • Tools are designed to connect, so the output of one can feed the next
  • Cover the main data situations, from catch-only to length-based and multispecies
Limitations
  • Adoption requires change from familiar tools and some retraining
  • Not every data situation is covered yet, for example age-structured models and simulation are planned
  • Results may differ from those of established methods during a transition
  • Apps and documentation need continuing support from the toolbox team

Neither group is wrong in itself. Established tools remain mapped in the figure below to show connections, and regional hubs discuss when to keep, extend or change them.

FAO tools

Tools are grouped by main purpose. Each lists the data it needs, what it produces, and the resources available for it. JABBA3 provides CMSY+, SPiCT and BSM, and FLicc provides LBSPR and LBB; these, with TropFishR and the age-structured models, are documented under catalogue entries. Resource lists are provisional and are to be confirmed with the toolbox team.
