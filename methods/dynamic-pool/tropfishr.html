Overview

Field Entry
Family / class Dynamic pool models → equilibrium per-recruit (length-based, with growth estimation)
Method name TropFishR 1.6.6 (R, CRAN; GPL-3): tokami/TropFishR · manual
Short description Compilation of length-frequency methods for data-poor fisheries, implementing the FAO manual of Sparre and Venema (1998) and newer methods: growth from length frequencies (ELEFAN with genetic algorithm or simulated annealing), mortality from length-converted catch curves, selectivity, VPA and per-recruit and yield models.
Main outputs Growth parameters (\(L_\infty\), \(K\), \(t_{anchor}\), seasonal \(C\), \(t_s\)); \(Z\), \(M\), \(F = Z - M\); selectivity (\(L_{50}\), \(L_{75}\)); YPR and SBPR curves with \(F_{max}\), \(F_{0.1}\), \(F_{0.5}\) and current exploitation status.
Typical use case Tropical and data-poor fisheries with length-frequency samples and no reliable age data, typically a single gear.

1 · Data

Field Entry
Minimum data A length-frequency matrix: catches in numbers by length class (rows) and sampling date (columns), with the vector of sampling dates (lfqCreate()).
Preferred data Monthly or more frequent samples over at least a year, covering all sizes; some direct age readings to check growth.
Optional auxiliary data Water temperature and \(L_{max}\) for empirical \(M\) (M_empirical()); length–weight parameters; gear selectivity experiments (select_Millar(), gillnetfit()).
Data quality considerations To be completed
Highlights representativeness, consistency and screening needs.
Pre-processing steps lfqCreate() from raw lengths, lfqModify() to aggregate bins, years or plus groups, lfqRestructure() (moving-average restructuring used by ELEFAN).
Standardization needs To be completed
Identifies whether indices or samples require standardization.
Aggregation / binning choices Bin width and moving-average span (MA) strongly affect ELEFAN; aggregate by year or quarter consistently.
Fleet / gear handling To be completed
Explains whether fleets or gears should be split or combined.
Missing data handling To be completed
Describes treatment of gaps and partial information.

Data template: length_long.csv and life_history.csv. Prepare the data first with the data preparation workflow.

library(TropFishR)
data(synLFQ7)
lfq <- lfqModify(synLFQ7, bin_size = 4)
fit <- ELEFAN_GA(lfq, MA = 5, seasonalised = FALSE,
                 low_par = list(Linf = 70, K = 0.1, t_anchor = 0),
                 up_par  = list(Linf = 110, K = 1, t_anchor = 1))
Ms  <- M_empirical(Linf = fit$par$Linf, K_l = fit$par$K, method = "Then_growth")
lfq2 <- lfqModify(lfqRestructure(lfq, MA = 5), par = list(Linf = fit$par$Linf, K = fit$par$K))
cc  <- catchCurve(lfq2, reg_int = NULL, calc_ogive = TRUE)   # Z and selectivity

Settings are illustrative; check argument names against the manual.

2 · Method

Field Entry
Software / tools install.packages("TropFishR"); Stock Monitoring Tool (ELEFAN_GA and YPR/SBPR, TropFishR 1.6.6).
Implementation notes ELEFAN restructures the length frequencies (bin scores relative to a moving average), scores growth curves by the bins they cross, and searches for the VBGF parameters with the highest score; growth is more reliable when at least some direct age readings are available.
Key parameters \(L_\infty\), \(K\), \(t_{anchor}\), \(C\), \(t_s\); \(M\); selectivity; \(L_c\) or \(t_c\).
Priors / fixed inputs Search ranges for growth parameters (low_par, up_par); \(L_\infty\) starting value from powell_wetherall() or \(L_{max}\).
Error structure None formal: ELEFAN maximises a score; catch curve by regression.
State-space formulation No.
Core assumptions Length frequencies represent the population (all ages, areas and habitats sampled); growth follows a (seasonal) von Bertalanffy curve; mortality and recruitment are constant (equilibrium).

3 · Outputs, diagnostics and sensitivities

Field Entry
Main sensitivities Moving-average span and bin width; search ranges for \(L_\infty\) and \(K\); \(M\) estimator; seasonal growth on or off.
Likely violations Emigration from the fishing grounds (overestimates Z); strong sexual dimorphism in size (biases growth); juveniles and adults in different habitats with sampling in only one of them.
Expected bias Wrong growth parameters propagate into \(Z\), \(M\) and status; under emigration \(Z\) is overestimated.
Warning signs ELEFAN score surface flat or with several peaks; \(L_\infty\) at a search bound; growth curves not following visible modes.

Diagnostics: apply the model acceptance checklist (convergence, residuals, prior vs posterior, retrospective, hindcast, jitter, plausibility) before results are used for advice.

Use and misuse

WarningMisuse warnings shown by the method selector
  • Caution: when goal = HCR testing: An assessment fit alone does not test a harvest control rule. Use the assessment as the estimation model in an MSE (see FLR mse).
  • Caution: when gears = multiple: ELEFAN and the catch curve assume one selectivity; pooled gears blur the growth modes and the catch curve. Analyse a single gear.
  • Caution: when lifehistory = long-lived: ELEFAN needs clear modal progression; for slow-growing, long-lived species the length modes overlap and growth estimates are unreliable. Prefer independent growth estimates.
  • Caution: when goal = management options: An assessment fit alone does not compare management options. Use it to condition an operating model and compare options in simulation (FPAT App for screening, FLR mse for HCR testing).

Rules are maintained in data/misuse_rules.csv.

Field Entry
Pre-run checks Is the length-frequency sample representative of the whole population? Are all age groups sampled? Were samples taken across the areas where different life stages live (e.g. juveniles nearshore, adults offshore)? Were gears with different selectivity used, so that several age groups appear? If not, F estimates will be biased (after Gutierrez and Kleisner, 2018).
Do not use when Length samples come from one habitat or gear that misses part of the population; modes are not visible (slow growth, long-lived species); several gears with different selectivity are pooled.
Common misuses Accepting the first ELEFAN solution without checking the score surface; using one empirical \(M\); reading \(F_{current} > F_{0.1}\) as precise status.
Pre-use checklist To be completed
Minimum checks to pass before results are used for advice.
Advice suitability F-based status relative to per-recruit reference points (\(F_{0.1}\) preferred to \(F_{max}\)); no biomass or catch level.

Case studies and references

Field Entry
Applications / case studies Example datasets in the package (synLFQ1–synLFQ8, alba, goatfish, hake and others); the Stock Monitoring Tool length-based workflow.
References Mildenberger, T.K., Taylor, M.H. & Wolff, M. (2017) Methods in Ecology and Evolution 8: 1520–1527; Sparre, P. & Venema, S.C. (1998) Introduction to tropical fish stock assessment. FAO Fisheries Technical Paper 306/1; Gutierrez, N.L. and Kleisner, K. (2018).

Suitable example

Synthetic data synLFQ7 with known growth: recover \(L_\infty\) and \(K\) and check against the truth.

Assumption sensitivity

ELEFAN results across moving-average spans (MA) and bin sizes; alternative empirical \(M\) estimators.

Unsuitable example

Samples from one gear or habitat only (e.g. adults offshore), or a slow-growing species without visible modes.
