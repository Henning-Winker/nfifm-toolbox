Overview

The Fisheries Performance Assessment Toolkit (FPAT) was developed by FAO with the University of Washington and Blue Matter Science under the GEF Coastal Fisheries Initiative, and piloted on ten fisheries in Cabo Verde, Côte d’Ivoire, Ecuador, Indonesia and Senegal (FAO Technical Paper 737). It runs as a four-step cycle:

1 · Benchmarking: FPI and governance scores → 2 · Planning: FPAT App MSE → 3 · Decision-making → 4 · Monitoring, then repeat

This page covers step 2, the FPAT App: a Shiny app that builds an operating model from the FPAT workbook and projects the stock under alternative management procedures, so that stakeholders can see how catch limits, effort limits, size limits or closures are likely to change abundance and catch. The manual describes it as a fishery management calculator. Step 1 is documented under Fishery Performance Indicators.

Field Entry
Family / class Management strategy evaluation → Rapid participatory MSE
Method name FPAT App (openMSE)
Short description Closed-loop simulation of a single stock under a fixed menu of management procedures, conditioned on catch, life-history and fishery information from the FPAT workbook.
Main outputs Historical reconstruction of the stock; projected catch and spawning biomass relative to \(B_{MSY}\) and current levels; trade-off plots across management procedures
Typical use case Workshops with scientists and technical managers that compare feasible measures identified in the FPI/governance benchmarking
Place in the toolbox Screening end of the Advice and MSE pillar. Formal testing of a harvest control rule against a set of operating models is done with FLR mse; joint advice in mixed fisheries with mmsyFM.

1 · Data

Field Entry
Minimum data Catch history and the core life-history parameters marked in red on the “Fishery Data” sheet (TP 737, Table 4), plus all ten “FPAT App Questions”.
Preferred data Abundance index with CV, mean length or catch-at-length, local estimates of growth, \(M\) and maturity, recent depletion from an assessment.
Optional auxiliary data Effort series, additional indices with vulnerability schedules, recruitment index, catch-at-age, reference catch or index levels, previous TAC/TAE.
Data quality considerations Catch completeness (App Question 9), proportionality of the index (Question 10), changes in effort and efficiency (Questions 1–2). These answers become uncertainty in the operating model, so they should be agreed with the people who know the fishery.
Pre-processing steps Run the data preparation workflow first; TP 737 Chapter 4 covers landings, price and revenue series, and life-history proxies (FishBase, related stocks in the region, Annex 4).
Standardization needs Use a standardised index (cpueTargeting); nominal CPUE with efficiency creep biases the reconstruction.
Fleet / gear handling One aggregate fleet with one selectivity curve (length at first capture, full selection, vulnerability at \(L_\infty\)).
Missing data handling Blank cells are left empty or set to NA; missing biological parameters are filled with wide priors, which widens the uncertainty.

Data template: the FPAT Excel workbook, sheets 13 “Fishery Data” and 14 “FPAT App Questions” (FPAT input file on GitHub; FPI scoresheets at fpilab.org).

The ten App Questions turn local knowledge into operating-model assumptions:

# Question Enters the operating model as
1 How variable has historic fishing effort been? Inter-annual variability in effort
2 Has fishing efficiency changed? Trend in catchability (effort creep)
3 What fraction of the catch is discarded? Discard rate
4 What fraction of discards die? Post-release mortality
5 Mixing between open and closed areas (existing closures) Movement between areas
6 Size of planned closures Area closed under the closure procedure
7 Mixing expected with planned closures Movement under the closure procedure
8 Which measures are feasible (TAC, TAE, size limits, time-area closures)? Which management procedures are offered
9 Are catches reported accurately? Implementation and reporting bias
10 Is the main index proportional to biomass? Hyperstability or hyperdepletion of the index

Questions 2, 8 and 10 are also asked in the method selector, under Management context.

2 · Method

Field Entry
Software / tools R package FPAT (Shiny app, v0.2.0, github.com/Blue-Matter/FPAT) built on openMSE (DLMtool, MSEtool); online version linked from TP 737 at fisheriesperformancetoolkit.com
Run locally remotes::install_github("Blue-Matter/FPAT"); FPAT::FPAT()
Management procedures offered Current catch; current effort; two size limits (based on length at maturity); two length-targeting rules; two index-targeting rules; keep existing areas open or close the planned area
Implementation notes The workbook is uploaded, the app builds and conditions an operating model, shows the FPI panels and the historical dynamics, then projects the selected procedures.
Priors / fixed inputs Means and CVs from the “Fishery Data” sheet; App Questions set the ranges of effort, discard, mixing, reporting and index-behaviour assumptions. In the current code, initial depletion is assumed to be 1 (the stock starts unfished at the beginning of the catch series).
Error structure Process and observation error sampled across simulations (openMSE)
State-space formulation n/a (simulation)
Core assumptions Single stock and species; one aggregate fleet; the catch series starts close to unfished; life-history parameters and their CVs bracket the truth.

3 · Outputs, diagnostics and sensitivities

Field Entry
Main outputs Projections of catch and abundance by management procedure; trade-off plots (e.g. yield against probability of being above \(B_{MSY}\), catch variability).
Main sensitivities Depletion at the start of the catch series; \(M\) and steepness; catch reporting bias; effort creep; index behaviour.
Likely violations Fishery already depleted when the catch series starts; incomplete catches; several fleets with different selectivity.
Expected bias Starting unfished when the stock was already fished overestimates productivity and current status; under-reported catches underestimate scale.
Warning signs Historical reconstruction at odds with local knowledge or the FPI stock scores; projections dominated by the prior ranges; very wide envelopes across all procedures.

Use and misuse

WarningMisuse warnings shown by the method selector
  • Caution: when goal = HCR testing: An assessment fit alone does not test a harvest control rule. Use the assessment as the estimation model in an MSE (see FLR mse).
  • Caution: when goal = management options: An assessment fit alone does not compare management options. Use it to condition an operating model and compare options in simulation (FPAT App for screening, FLR mse for HCR testing).
  • Caution: when catch = incomplete: The FPAT operating model is reconstructed from the catch series. Correct for under-reporting first (App Question 9) or the projected yields and abundance are biased.
  • Caution: when species = mixed: The FPAT App runs one species at a time. In a mixed fishery, run the main species separately and use mmsyFM for joint reference points and effort allocation.
  • Caution: when goal = HCR testing: The FPAT App compares a fixed menu of management procedures with limited conditioning. For formal testing of a harvest control rule use FLR mse.

Rules are maintained in data/misuse_rules.csv.

Field Entry
Pre-run checks (1) Does the catch series start near the beginning of the fishery? If not, the start-unfished assumption must be revisited. (2) Are the life-history parameters local, or proxies with CVs wide enough? (3) Have the App Questions been answered with harvesters and managers, not only by the analyst? (4) Are the management procedures in the run the ones flagged as feasible in Question 8?
Do not use when The outcome is a formally adopted harvest control rule (use FLR mse with an agreed set of operating models); the fishery is strongly mixed and the joint outcome matters (use mmsyFM).
Common misuses Reading projections as forecasts rather than comparisons between options; reporting the reconstructed depletion as stock status; ignoring that results are biological only (economic and social consequences come from the FPI benchmarking).
Pre-use checklist Data reviewed and agreed in the workshop; historical reconstruction plausible; alternative datasets tried where there is disagreement; ranking of procedures robust to the main sensitivities.
Advice suitability Screening of management options and identification of data priorities; not a stand-alone basis for catch advice.

Case studies and references

Field Entry
Applications / case studies Coastal Fisheries Initiative workshops in West Africa (Cabo Verde, Côte d’Ivoire, Senegal), Indonesia and Latin America (TP 737, Chapter 6): comparisons of size limits with the status quo; data-collection priorities; feasibility of TACs given monitoring and enforcement.
Workshop format Three-day MSE workshop for 4–12 scientists and technical managers after the assessment workshop; see Training and apps.
References De Alessi, M., Anderson, C., Carruthers, T., Hordyk, A., Sock, F. & Gutierrez, N.L. (2026). The Fisheries Performance Assessment Toolkit (FPAT): a practical manual. FAO Fisheries and Aquaculture Technical Paper 737. Rome, FAO (in press).
Hordyk, A., Huynh, Q. & Carruthers, T. (2025). MSEtool: Management Strategy Evaluation Toolkit. R package, https://msetool.openmse.com/.

Suitable example

A single-species, single-gear coastal fishery with a catch history from the start of the fishery, where stakeholders want to compare a size limit with current catch and current effort.

Assumption sensitivity

Catch records start after decades of unrecorded fishing: rerun with lower initial depletion and compare the ranking of procedures.

Unsuitable example

A multispecies trawl fishery where the question is a joint effort cap across species and fleets.
